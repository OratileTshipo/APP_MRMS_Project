Prepared For: Deputy Director Monitoring and Evaluation – End User

1. Purpose of this Document
The BRD/FDS/TDS produced so far describe the system as we have designed and are building it. They have not yet been validated against the people who will actually use it day to day, or against the one person whose role changes most: the Deputy Director: Monitoring &amp; Evaluation, who is moving from sole reviewer of every report to a cross-cutting escalation authority sitting above a new Supervisor tier.
This document has two purposes:
Capture User Requirements (URS) in the stakeholders' own words, independent of the schema/screens already built, so we can check the build actually matches the need rather than the other way around.
Provide a structured interview/validation guide for a short round of stakeholder conversations — with a dedicated deep-dive for the Deputy Director: M&amp;E — before we lock down Phase 1 sign-off and commit further Phase 2 build effort.
⚠ Recommended sequencing: run the Deputy Director: M&amp;E session first. If the escalation model doesn't hold up, it changes assumptions for every other role's requirements below.
2. Confirmed Core Hierarchy (corrected)
Programme (1) └─ Directorate (Many) └─ Project (Many) └─ Activity (Many) └─ MonthlyReport (Many)
Programme is the top-level grouping (4 program budget structures); each Directorate sits within one Programme; Projects, Activities, and Reports cascade beneath. Note for the schema: Directorates.Programme is currently a text field rather than a Lookup — functionally correct direction, but not yet referentially enforced (tracked as an open item in TDS §3.13).

5. Deep-Dive Interview Guide — Deputy Director: Monitoring &amp; Evaluation
This session matters more than any other single input to this project. The current design removes the Deputy Director from the first line of review — that is a significant change to how M&amp;E currently exercises control, and it needs explicit, informed buy-in rather than passive acceptance of a diagram.
Logistics: 45–60 minutes, in person or video call, Oratile Tshipo + Deputy Director: M&amp;E only (avoid a large group for this one — candour matters more than optics).
5.1 Warm-up &amp; current-state grounding (10 min)
Q1. Walk me through what happens today, from when a directorate submits their monthly Excel tab, to when you've finished reviewing it. Where does most of your time actually go?
Q2. Of everything you currently review, roughly what proportion do you think a capable Director/Deputy Director could reasonably sign off on themselves, without it coming to you first?
↳ Probe: Listen for: is the bottleneck about volume, about quality-control distrust, about accountability/liability, or about needing the full picture before anything is 'final'? Each implies a different fix.
5.2 Reaction to the proposed model (15 min)
Q3. Here is the proposed model: each Directorate's Director/Deputy Director/Delegate reviews their own directorate's submissions first. You retain the ability to view and act on anything, at any time, across all directorates. What's your honest first reaction?
Q4. What would make you NOT trust a Supervisor's approval — i.e. what would still make you want to check it yourself even after they've signed off?
↳ Probe: This directly informs whether we need a 'DDME review required' flag on certain report types/directorates, rather than a blanket after-the-fact escalation right.
Q5. If a Supervisor approves something you would have rejected, how do you want to find out — immediately, at your own review cadence, or only if something goes wrong later (e.g. an audit finding)?
Q6. Are there specific directorates, projects, or types of activity (e.g. high budget, high political visibility, recurring AGSA findings) where you want mandatory sign-off from you regardless of Supervisor approval?
↳ Probe: If yes, this is a real functional requirement we have not yet built — an escalation rule engine, not just a blanket role permission.
5.3 What you actually need to do your job (15 min)
Q7. What does a 'red flag' look like to you today — late submission, low percentage complete, a rejected-then-resubmitted report, a Supervisor sitting on something too long? Which of these matters most?
Q8. How do you currently report up to the HoD/DMC/EMC? What format, what frequency, and what would 'good' look like coming out of this system instead?
Q9. For audit purposes (AGSA or internal), what evidence do you currently have to keep, and what's missing today that this system should capture?
↳ Probe: Ties directly to AuditLog/ReportComments — confirm what 'who changed what and why' detail is actually required vs. nice-to-have.
Q10. If a Director consistently fails to review on time, what do you need the system to show you to manage that performance conversation?
5.4 Trust, adoption, and change management (10 min)
Q11. Realistically, how will the 17 Directors/Deputy Directors/Delegates react to suddenly having review responsibility they didn't have before? Who do you expect to resist, and why?
Q12. Is there anything about giving up first-line review that feels like a loss of control you're not actually comfortable with, even if it's operationally sensible?
↳ Probe: Ask this plainly and give room for an honest answer — a diplomatic "yes, in principle" now and quiet workaround-by-email later is the outcome we most want to avoid.
Q13. What would you need to see in a pilot (e.g. with ICTM, which is furthest along) before you'd be comfortable recommending this roll out to all 17 directorates?
5.5 Wrap-up (5 min)
Q14. Is there anything about how M&amp;E works today that this conversation hasn't touched on, that you'd want reflected in this system?
Thank them. Confirm next steps: findings will be reflected back into the URS sign-off table (§6) and, where they change the approval model, into an updated FDS.
